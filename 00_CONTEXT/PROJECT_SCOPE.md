# Project scope and limits

## Project context
- Reference donor: Suzuki EN125; exact year/variant remains to be confirmed.
- Implementation location: Mauritius.
- Objective: develop an evidence-led path to a functioning electric conversion, with engineering feasibility and build planning progressing independently from statutory road-use approval.

## Included
- Engineering requirements and traceability.
- EN125 donor baseline and measurement plan.
- Evidence review of completed motorcycle conversions and published sizing methods.
- Concept-level electric powertrain and vehicle architecture.
- Mechanical, electrical, battery, embedded-control and safety design bases.
- Mauritius workshop/supplier qualification and resilient procurement.
- Prototype verification planning, cost modelling and professional portfolio presentation.
- Separate statutory, registration and insurance research for any eventual road use.

## Excluded until separately approved
- Instructions for unsupervised live high-voltage assembly or energisation.
- Unsupervised traction-battery construction, repair or modification.
- Uncontrolled vehicle testing or passenger/cargo operation.
- Claims of legal approval, certification, roadworthiness, range, performance or savings.
- Final selection of motor, controller, battery, charger, fuse, contactor or cable without system calculations and competent review.

## Required inputs
1. Exact Suzuki EN125 year, variant, frame/engine documentation and condition.
2. Intended operating profile: route, distance, gradient, speed, payload, duty cycle and charging access.
3. Baseline mass, axle loads, wheel/tyre/brake dimensions and frame measurements.
4. Mauritius-based mechanical, fabrication and electrical capability.
5. Specialist traction motor/controller and battery supplier evidence.
6. Competent mechanical/electrical reviewers and a controlled test pathway.

## Decision principle
Engineering feasibility, packaging, costing and supplier discovery should not be blocked by unresolved road-use approval. However, structural integrity, battery protection, electrical isolation, braking, thermal limits and controlled testing are engineering requirements, not optional legal formalities. Public-road operation remains a separate statutory and insurance release gate.
