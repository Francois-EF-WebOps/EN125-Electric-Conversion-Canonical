# Procurement strategy — resilient, evidence-led sourcing

## Principle
Procure by verified specification, not marketplace keyword or lowest price. A critical component is not approved until its exact model, ratings, interface, datasheet, compliance evidence, warranty and compatibility are checked.

## Sourcing layers
1. Local fabrication and ordinary mechanical parts: obtain multiple quotes; retain drawings, material evidence and inspection records.
2. Local low-voltage electronics and instrumentation: use established Thai electronics retailers for prototyping, sensors, connectors and non-traction test equipment. These parts are not automatically suitable for vehicle safety functions.
3. Specialist traction components: obtain direct manufacturer datasheets and written compatibility from motor/controller suppliers; require exact model and revision.
4. Battery and charger: prefer traceable pack manufacturers or qualified integrators able to provide cell provenance, pack test evidence, BMS documentation, charger compatibility and service/warranty route.
5. External procurement: use manufacturer-authorised channels where possible; use import marketplaces only when seller identity, exact model, technical documents, customs/shipping constraints and return/warranty path are verifiable.
6. Second source: identify a technically interchangeable alternate before design freeze. If no safe alternate exists, record single-source risk and a repair/replacement plan.

## Supplier qualification record
Record legal business name, address and contact source; manufacturer/reseller/fabricator role; exact part/model/revision; datasheet and date; certificate/test report and issuer; written compatibility response; stock, quote date, currency, shipping, import/VAT/duty and lead time; warranty, returns, repair and spare parts; batch/serial traceability; primary/alternate status.

## No-go signals
No datasheet; inconsistent ratings; no cell/pack provenance; BMS/charger compatibility undocumented; seller refuses exact model/serial; “waterproof” claim without rating/test basis; no warranty/return route; substitution without system compatibility review.

## External procurement resilience
- Maintain generic interface specifications for motor, controller, battery and charger.
- Prefer manufacturer documentation and authorised distributors.
- Require applicable battery transport documents, including UN 38.3 test summary where relevant to transport; this is not vehicle safety certification.
- Record customs/import treatment and dangerous-goods shipping constraints before ordering.
- Do not buy cells, packs or controllers until ratings and integration requirements are frozen.
- Keep two sources for ordinary consumables and a documented service path for safety-critical proprietary items.

## Immediate procurement state
No traction components are approved for purchase. Any early low-voltage test equipment purchase must follow a defined test objective and instrument safety class. Do not buy a motor, battery, controller or charger against the current incomplete baseline.
