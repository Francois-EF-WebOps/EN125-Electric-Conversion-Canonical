# Procurement strategy — Mauritius-first, evidence-led sourcing

## Principle
Procure by verified specification, not marketplace keyword or lowest price. A critical component is not approved until its exact model, ratings, datasheet, test evidence, compatibility, warranty and service path are checked.

## Sourcing layers
1. Local motorcycle workshop: donor inspection, teardown, fitment, final assembly and service access.
2. Local fabrication/machining: mounts, brackets, enclosure and precision parts manufactured from reviewed drawings with material and inspection records.
3. Local electrical capability: low-voltage wiring, diagnostics, instrumentation and charging infrastructure; do not assume stationary solar-storage experience equals traction-battery engineering.
4. Specialist traction components: obtain exact motor/controller models, manufacturer datasheets and written compatibility from the supplier.
5. Battery and charger: prefer a qualified pack manufacturer/integrator able to provide cell provenance, pack test evidence, BMS documentation, charger compatibility and service/warranty route.
6. External procurement: compare landed cost, freight, dangerous-goods shipping, customs, tax, warranty returns and spare-part lead time before ordering.
7. Second source: identify a technically interchangeable alternate before design freeze. If no safe alternate exists, record single-source risk and a repair/replacement plan.

## Supplier qualification record
Record legal business name, address and contact source; manufacturer/reseller/fabricator role; exact part/model/revision; datasheet and date; certificate/test report and issuer; written compatibility response; stock, quote date, currency, shipping, import/tax and lead time; warranty, returns, repair and spare parts; batch/serial traceability; primary/alternate status.

## No-go signals
- No exact model or datasheet.
- Inconsistent voltage/current ratings.
- No cell/pack provenance.
- BMS/charger compatibility undocumented.
- No test evidence or warranty/return path.
- “Waterproof” claim without rating/test basis.
- Substitution without system compatibility review.
- A supplier claiming complete conversion capability but unable to identify a comparable completed project or explain the design/test process.

## Procurement order
1. Confirm donor and mission profile.
2. Complete road-load, motor/ratio and battery sizing.
3. Verify packaging with non-energised mockups.
4. Obtain written matched-system proposals and local fabrication quotes.
5. Compare landed cost and lifecycle serviceability.
6. Complete technical review.
7. Release purchase orders by controlled BOM revision.

## Immediate procurement state
