# Cabo Verde context and use-case framing

**Status:** preliminary desk-research summary. Verify each current policy, infrastructure, and market statement before using it for a decision.

## Why this is relevant

Cabo Verde is an island country where transport fuel imports, inter-island geography, local service access, and maintenance logistics may matter to transport economics. Those factors make a locally grounded comparison useful; they do **not** by themselves prove that a converted motorcycle is the best solution.

The Government of Cabo Verde's climate portal reports that:
- national policy targets carbon neutrality by 2050 and at least 50% renewable-energy penetration by 2030;
- road transport accounts for a substantial share of imported fossil-fuel use and national emissions;
- the Nationally Determined Contribution includes a target of at least 25% of the national vehicle fleet being electric by 2030;
- the Electric Mobility Policy Charter was approved in 2019;
- the ProMEC programme supported charging infrastructure, incentives, regulatory development, and training; and
- public reporting described 40 public charging stations across inhabited islands and growing electric-vehicle uptake.

These are policy- and programme-level indicators, not evidence that a particular conversion is lawful, safe, serviceable, affordable, or compatible with a particular island's charging network. Re-check current status, dates, definitions, and island-level availability in the linked primary sources before relying on any figure.

## Define the use case before selecting technology

The first research target should be one **specific recurring task**, not “electrify island transport” in general.

Candidate use cases to investigate—not established recommendations:
- scheduled small-parcel or document delivery;
- repeated short-distance errands for a defined organisation;
- maintenance or service visits on a predictable route;
- campus, workshop, or closed-site movement where public-road requirements differ (but still require site-safety review).

For each candidate, collect:
1. island and municipality;
2. route or service area and elevation/surface conditions;
3. daily distance and stop count, including return-to-base travel;
4. payload and any passenger requirement;
5. time window, weather exposure, and days of operation;
6. secure overnight parking and charging access;
7. current transport method and observed operating cost;
8. named operator/customer and evidence of actual demand;
9. who will maintain the vehicle and source replacement parts;
10. relevant legal route for the exact vehicle and use.

Do not publish a specific route or customer name without permission.

## Alternatives to compare

At minimum compare:
- continue using the existing petrol vehicle;
- purchase or lease a factory-built electric two-wheeler, if locally available and suitable;
- convert an existing vehicle, only if lawful and technically supportable;
- use a bicycle, cargo bicycle, walking, shared delivery, or public transport where the use case permits;
- consolidate trips or change dispatch schedules to reduce distance.

A conversion should not win by excluding the cost of engineering, inspection, downtime, compliance, battery replacement, or the value of skilled labour.

## Local evidence required

| Topic | Required evidence | Do not assume |
|---|---|---|
| Charging | Location, access rules, uptime, connector, price, opening hours, permission for commercial use | A national station count means a usable charger is nearby |
| Electricity | Applicable tariff, charging location, meter arrangement, losses, reliability | Household and commercial rates are identical |
| Vehicle market | Actual local quotes, warranty, service network, import duties/taxes, lead time | Global online prices equal landed local cost |
| Roads and climate | Route observations, slope, surface, rain, heat, corrosion exposure | A laboratory range applies to local duty |
| Demand | Named buyer/operator, route log, frequency, service-level expectation | General interest equals paid demand |
| Skills | Local training provider, competent reviewer, workshop capability | A training document proves competence |
| End of life | Battery handling, transport, storage, collection/recycling pathway | A used battery has a guaranteed resale value |

## Climate and energy claims

Electric mobility can reduce direct tailpipe emissions, but lifecycle impacts depend on electricity supply, vehicle manufacture, battery replacement, utilisation, and the displaced vehicle. Report direct emissions, electricity use, and lifecycle assumptions separately. Do not turn a national renewable-energy target into a claim that charging is currently 100% renewable.
