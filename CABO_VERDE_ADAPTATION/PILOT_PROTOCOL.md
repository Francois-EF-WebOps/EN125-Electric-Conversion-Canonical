# Pilot protocol: from desk study to operational evidence

**Current status: Stage 0 only.** No conversion, road test, or commercial operation is authorised by this document.

## Stage 0 — Desk study and use-case definition

Deliverables:
- named island/municipality and bounded use case;
- route/duty-cycle observations or a lawful baseline log;
- evidence of a real recurring transport need;
- existing transport baseline;
- local charger/access and electricity evidence;
- comparable local quotations;
- preliminary legal route and competent reviewer identified;
- risk register and decision owner.

**Exit criteria:** all essential inputs are sourced or explicitly marked OPEN. If there is no credible demand or no plausible lawful path, stop or choose a different transport option.

## Stage 1 — Legal and engineering feasibility

Deliverables:
- written regulatory determination for the exact configuration and use;
- insurer's written position;
- competent design review and standards/inspection plan;
- battery handling and emergency plan;
- preliminary parts, maintenance, and end-of-life plan;
- updated cost model.

**Exit criteria:** no unresolved critical legal or safety blocker. If the requirements cannot be met within available resources, do not build.

## Stage 2 — Non-road technical validation

Only if Stage 1 is passed and the test setup is authorised.

Deliverables:
- documented build configuration and component traceability;
- inspection and non-energised checks;
- controlled test plan, risk assessment, and abort criteria;
- instrumented results for the intended duty cycle;
- fault log and corrective actions;
- independent review of results.

Testing is to be designed and supervised by competent people. This document does not supply wiring diagrams, component ratings, or a safe conversion recipe.

**Exit criteria:** every critical test passes, results are repeatable, and required independent sign-off is recorded. A critical fault means stop, isolate, investigate, and re-review before any retest.

## Stage 3 — Restricted operational pilot

Only after legal road-use, inspection, insurance, and operational gates are passed in writing.

Set the duration and sample size based on the real use case before starting. Record:
- scheduled and completed jobs;
- energy per kilometre and per job;
- route, payload, and conditions;
- service availability and missed jobs;
- maintenance time, parts availability, faults, and downtime;
- total cost and cost per job;
- operator training, incidents, near misses, and complaints.

Do not carry passengers or loads beyond the approved configuration or operate outside validated conditions. Protect personal, route, and customer data.

## Stage 4 — Decision

Publish a concise result that includes:
- question and scope;
- dates and sample size;
- configuration and limitations;
- results, including failed tests and missing data;
- comparison with baseline and alternatives;
- safety/legal review status;
- total cost and sensitivity analysis;
- worker compensation and hours;
- battery end-of-life plan;
- go / adapt / stop decision and reasons.

A small pilot cannot establish general reliability, lifecycle emissions, national suitability, or broad job-creation impact.

## Stop-work triggers

Stop the test or operation and make the system safe if any of the following occurs:
- unexpected battery heating, swelling, smoke, smell, leakage, or damage;
- electrical shock, arcing, exposed conductor, repeated protection trip, or unexplained fault;
- loss of braking, steering, lighting, secure mounting, or other safety-critical function;
- water ingress or impact beyond the approved design conditions;
- abnormal noise, vibration, tyre, suspension, or handling behaviour;
- a legal, insurance, inspection, or operating condition is no longer met;
- an incident, near miss, or result that contradicts the risk assessment.

Follow the site emergency plan and local emergency services guidance. Do not handle a damaged battery without trained personnel and appropriate procedures.
