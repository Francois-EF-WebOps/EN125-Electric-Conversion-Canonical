# Regulatory and safety gate

**Rule:** no public-road use, passenger carriage, or commercial delivery by a converted vehicle until each applicable gate is resolved in writing by the appropriate competent party.

This is a research checklist, not legal advice. The exact requirements depend on the current consolidated Cabo Verde laws, vehicle category, modification scope, registration status, intended use, and location.

## Gate 0 — Identify the exact configuration

Record:
- donor make/model/year and exact variant;
- frame/chassis and registration identifiers (keep private in public artifacts);
- current registration and roadworthiness status;
- proposed motor, controller, battery chemistry/configuration, charger, and mounting approach;
- total mass, axle loads, tyres, brakes, suspension, lighting, and intended payload;
- whether the vehicle will be used on public roads, private premises, or both;
- whether passengers or paid/commercial carriage are involved.

No generic “EN125 conversion” approval can be inferred for all model years or build configurations.

## Gate 1 — Written regulatory determination

Obtain the applicable current instruments and a written answer, from a competent Cabo Verde authority or qualified local professional, to each question:

- Can this category of vehicle be modified in this way?
- Is prior authorisation, engineering documentation, homologation/type approval, inspection, or re-registration required?
- Which vehicle category and technical standards apply after modification?
- What documentation is required for motor, battery, charger, wiring, brakes, lights, and mass changes?
- Are there import/customs/tax requirements for the donor, conversion kit, motor, battery, and charger?
- Are commercial-use, delivery, passenger, or municipal permissions applicable?
- What inspection and periodic roadworthiness requirements apply?
- What evidence must be retained and presented during inspection?

**Stop condition:** if the modification route is unclear, unavailable, or denied, do not proceed to road-use conversion. Investigate a factory-built approved EV or a non-road research rig instead.

## Gate 2 — Insurance and liability

Before any operational test, obtain written confirmation from the insurer that the exact modified configuration and intended use are covered. Confirm third-party liability, commercial carriage, theft, battery/fire damage, workshop work, and test activity as relevant.

A standard policy on the unmodified donor must not be assumed to cover a conversion. Do not describe a vehicle as insured based only on an unmodified policy.

## Gate 3 — Competent engineering review

A qualified vehicle/electrical engineer or appropriately competent conversion professional must review the specific design, including:
- mechanical mounting and structural integrity;
- braking performance, tyres, suspension, mass distribution, and handling;
- electrical isolation, overcurrent protection, cable routing, connectors, enclosure, ingress, vibration, and abrasion;
- battery management, cell compatibility, temperature limits, secure retention, impact protection, and service disconnect;
- charger compatibility, earthing/protection arrangements, installation, and charging location;
- electromagnetic compatibility and interference with vehicle systems;
- lighting, indicators, horn, instrumentation, and emergency shutdown;
- safe maintenance, isolation, storage, transport, and end-of-life handling.

This list is a scope prompt, not a build procedure or substitute for standards-based review.

## Gate 4 — Controlled testing

Testing must be documented, supervised by competent personnel, conducted in an authorised safe area, and staged from non-energised checks to controlled low-risk trials. Define abort criteria in advance. Do not test in public traffic before legal permission and all safety gates are satisfied.

Record the build configuration, instruments, calibration status, test conditions, results, failures, repairs, and reviewer sign-off. A successful short test does not establish long-term durability or legal compliance.

## Gate 5 — Operational release

Before any real service:
- regulatory route resolved;
- insurance confirmed in writing;
- technical review and required inspections passed;
- charging site and battery procedures approved;
- operator trained and assessed;
- maintenance schedule and spare-parts pathway established;
- incident, fire, collision, breakdown, and recovery procedures in place;
- route, payload, weather, and duty cycle within validated limits;
- consent, privacy, and data-retention arrangements defined.

## Evidence record

For each gate, store:
- gate ID;
- exact question;
- responsible authority/reviewer role (not personal contact details in public records);
- instrument/report title and stable source;
- date and version;
- written determination or test reference;
- limitations and conditions;
- decision: PASS / FAIL / OPEN;
- next action and owner.

Never mark a gate PASS from a verbal assumption or a general policy statement.
